Vanishing and Exploding Gradients¶

Key Concepts¶

1. Vanishing and Exploding Gradients¶

Where they come from¶

  • During backpropagation each layer contributes a local derivative, and the chain rule multiplies them. The gradient for an early layer is a product of all the local derivatives between it and the loss.
  • Mostly < 1 → the gradient shrinks as it travels backward. Mostly > 1 → it grows rapidly.
flowchart TD
    A["Deep network multiplies<br/>many local derivatives"] --> B{"Typical size<br/>of each derivative"}
    B -->|"< 1"| C["Vanishing gradients<br/>early layers stop learning"]
    B -->|"> 1"| D["Exploding gradients<br/>huge, unstable updates"]

Vanishing gradients¶

  • Causes:
    • Saturating activations (sigmoid, tanh): derivatives become tiny.
    • Weights initialised too small: activations stay small, and so do the derivatives.
  • Numbers: each derivative ≈ 0.5, 20 layers → 0.5²⁰ ≈ 10⁻⁶, almost zero. Lower layers get almost no signal and stop learning.

Exploding gradients¶

  • Causes: weights too large, or activations that amplify inputs instead of controlling them.
  • Numbers: each layer multiplies by about 1.5, 20 layers → 1.5²⁰ ≈ 3300. Weight updates become enormous; the model becomes unstable or diverges.

Role of the activation function¶

Activation Derivative Effect
Sigmoid Never above 0.25; near 0 when saturated Strongly encourages vanishing gradients
Tanh Zero-centred, but still saturates (derivatives near 0) Still prone to vanishing
ReLU Either 0 or 1 Doesn't shrink gradients; but with large weights even ReLU networks can explode
  • ★ The choice of activation directly affects gradient flow.

Symptoms¶

Vanishing Exploding
First few layers barely change Loss suddenly spikes or becomes NaN
Training accuracy plateaus very early Weight values grow extremely large
Loss decreases extremely slowly Updates look chaotic and unstable
  • How to diagnose: watch the training loss across epochs. If it doesn't follow a stable pattern, suspect vanishing or exploding gradients, then inspect the gradient values layer by layer. Practical debugging comes later in the course.
  • ReLU avoids the vanishing problem, but its output is unbounded on the positive side, so with large weights it is the activation where exploding gradients typically show up. Gradient clipping and normalisation layers are the remedies for that.

2. Techniques to Mitigate Vanishing and Exploding Gradients¶

The four tools¶

flowchart LR
    A["Stable gradient flow"] --> B["Weight initialisation<br/>Xavier / He"]
    A --> C["Activation choice<br/>ReLU family"]
    A --> D["Gradient clipping"]
    A --> E["Normalisation layers<br/>batch norm / layer norm"]

1. Weight initialisation¶

  • Xavier (Glorot): keeps activation and gradient variance consistent across layers; works well with sigmoid and tanh.
  • He: the same idea scaled for ReLU-type activations: variance 2 / n_in, to compensate for ReLU blocking half the inputs.
  • Goal: keep the forward and backward signal from shrinking or exploding. Without good initialisation, the other techniques are much less effective.

2. Activation functions¶

  • Sigmoid/tanh saturate easily (derivatives near 0), which is why deep sigmoid networks used to be so hard to train.
  • ReLU has derivative 1 for positive inputs, so gradients pass cleanly. Leaky ReLU, GELU and other variants extend this idea.
  • ★ Using the right activation greatly reduces the vanishing-gradient risk, and is a big part of why deep learning became feasible.

3. Gradient clipping¶

  • Targets exploding gradients: if the gradient norm exceeds a preset limit, scale it down so updates stay controlled.
  • Widely used in RNNs, LSTMs and transformers. Think of it as a safety cap on gradient size.

4. Normalisation layers¶

Batch normalisation Layer normalisation
What it does Normalises activations within a batch Normalises at the feature level (per sample)
Where it is common Deep feed-forward and convolutional networks Transformers, where batch statistics aren't always reliable
  • Both keep activations in a healthy range, which keeps derivatives in a healthy range. Batch norm also reduces internal covariate shift and helps deeper networks train.
  • Stabilise both the forward and backward pass, helping against vanishing and exploding gradients.

Summary¶

Problem Main remedies
Vanishing Good initialisation, ReLU family, normalisation layers
Exploding Good initialisation, gradient clipping, normalisation layers
