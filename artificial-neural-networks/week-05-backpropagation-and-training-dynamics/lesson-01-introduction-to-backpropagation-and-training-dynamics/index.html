Introduction to Backpropagation and Training Dynamics¶

Key Concepts¶

The question this week answers¶

  • So far: network structure (MLPs, hidden layers, activation functions) and the forward pass that produces predictions.
  • Open question: how does a network improve itself using data? When it makes a mistake, how does it know what went wrong and what to change?
  • Training = adjusting the internal parameters (weights and biases) so predictions get better.

The training loop¶

flowchart LR
    A["Forward pass<br/>compute predictions"] --> B["Loss computation<br/>compare with reality"]
    B --> C["Gradients<br/>how much does each parameter<br/>contribute to the error?"]
    C --> D["Parameter update<br/>correct gradually"]
    D --> A
  • ★ Backpropagation is the mathematical process that computes the gradients: it shows how responsibility for the error is distributed across the whole network.
  • Four steps per training iteration: (1) forward pass with the current weights (random, from an initialisation method, or adjusted by an earlier update); (2) loss = a difference between true y and predicted ŷ; (3) backpropagation: attribute the error ("how much is w₁ responsible, how much w₂?"); (4) parameter update / optimisation: change the weights to reduce the error. This week covers steps 1–3; optimisation (step 4) is the next session.

What this week covers¶

  • What backpropagation actually computes.
  • How gradients flow through multiple layers.
  • Why the initial values of weights matter for stability.
  • How different loss functions influence learning.
  • Why deep networks suffer from vanishing and exploding gradients, and how to diagnose and mitigate them.

Conceptual shift¶

  • Earlier weeks: structure, representations, forward computation.
  • This week: adaptation, learning and optimisation. Forward computation, error signals and gradient-based parameter updates join into one learning process.
  • This is where the mathematics behind learning truly begins.
